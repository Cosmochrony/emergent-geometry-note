  \begin{abstract}
    The emergent geometry sub-programme of the Cosmochrony corpus addresses a single
    central question: how does the admissible fibre, on a supplied Heisenberg carrier, give rise
    to an effective four-dimensional Lorentzian geometry?
    Starting from the admissibility filter $\Pi_q$ acting on the supplied carrier
    $\mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ of the Heisenberg--Schr\"{o}dinger representation
    $\pi_c$ of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, the sub-programme organises the following chain:
    \[
      \Pi_q
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \Leff
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \geff
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}.
    \]
    This note maps twelve constituent papers (Q5a, Q5a-O2, Q5b, Q6b, Q7--Q11, U1, W1, H2)
    across five internal phases and records the status of every result.
    \emph{Status revision (version 2.0).}
    Version 2.0 corrects the status of the downstream results: the lifting hypothesis [H-lift]
    is open, the signature result concerns the non-degenerate block of a rank-three co-metric,
    no metric coefficient value is established, and the Heisenberg carrier is supplied rather
    than selected; withdrawn attributions are listed in Section~7.5.
    As recorded since version 1.1, Q5a version 3.0 withdraws the first link of this chain: the canonical filtration of the
    admissible fibre is exactly a growing toric Fourier window, the published admissibility
    form converges to the zero form on it (assuming uniformly bounded weights, which is
    numerically supported but not proved), and, under the unproved, numerically supported
    hypotheses [H-depth] (diverging saturation depth) and [H-$w'$] (positive finite weight
    limits), no common scalar normalisation produces a non-trivial toric differential operator.
    The existence of the spatial limit operator $\Lpi = -A\partial_x^2$ is now the explicit,
    unestablished hypothesis [H-L] (Q5b version 2.0), and every downstream result that
    consumes it is conditional on [H-L].
    The geometric convergence results (Carnot limit, $\Dhom = 4$) are independent of [H-L]
    and stand.
    No metric coefficient value is established: invariance fixes the shape of an invariant
    spatial form and leaves its scale free (Q8), leaves the temporal coefficient independent of
    the spatial one (Q11), and the isotropy route of Q10 rests on inputs that are not supplied;
    the value 2 is the eigenvalue of the $\su(2)$ Casimir on the spin-one module in one
    normalisation of the generators, not a coefficient.
    On the signature axis, Q5b establishes, conditionally, a rank-three co-metric whose
    non-degenerate block has signature $(-,+,+)$; the rank-four extension that the last link
    of the chain presupposes is not constructed (open problem Q5b-O2), and the endpoint
    $\geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}$ holds only for an extension satisfying the hypotheses of Q11
    Corollary~6.1 ([T] and [INV$^+$] of Q8, [ACT$_\tau$] invariance and a negative ordering term), after rescaling $\tau$, with its
    overall scale free.
    Question Q5 is open.
    \emph{Interpretive outlook (a reading, not a result).}
    If a spatial limit operator, the lifting step, the hyperbolicity hypothesis, a rank-four
    extension satisfying the hypotheses of Q11 Corollary~6.1 and an independent
    datum fixing the temporal-to-spatial ratio were all supplied, the chain would describe an
    effective spacetime reconstructed from admissibility rather than postulated; as it stands,
    it identifies which inputs such a reconstruction requires, and representation theory
    fixes the shapes of the invariant forms, not their sizes.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Motivation and Central Question}
    \label{sec:motivation}

    The emergent geometry sub-programme answers the following question.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Central question.}
    The admissibility filter $\Pi_q$ of the Cosmochrony framework acts on a supplied
    Heisenberg carrier, the Heisenberg--Schr\"{o}dinger representation $\pi_c$ of
    $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ on $\mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, selecting
    admissible modes according to the Born--Infeld bounded-flux constraint.
    \textit{What effective geometry is selected in the large-$q$ limit of this structure,
      and what determines the numerical values of its metric coefficients?}

    \medskip
    The practical importance of this question is that the entire gravitational, gauge, and
    fermionic content of Branch~III depends on the identification of an effective spacetime.
    Without a derived metric, the dynamical outputs of the Gravity paper~\cite{Beau2026h},
    the gauge structure of Q6a~\cite{Beau2026q6a}, and the spinorial structure of
    Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14} would each require an independently postulated background.
    The emergent geometry sub-programme aims to remove this postulate by reconstructing the
    effective geometry from admissibility rather than postulating it; the reconstruction is at
    present conditional and incomplete (Section~\ref{sec:status}).

    \medskip
    This note is concerned exclusively with the reconstruction of an effective metric of the
    form $\geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}$, a target that presupposes a rank-four extension of the
    co-metric (open problem Q5b-O2) and whose overall scale is not fixed
    (Section~\ref{sec:q7q11}).
    The dynamical content — whether, and under which inputs, the effective metric satisfies the Einstein
    equations — is the subject of the spectral gravity sub-programme and is not claimed here.

  \section{Position in the Cosmochrony Programme}
    \label{sec:position}

    The emergent geometry sub-programme occupies the interface between Branch~I (axiomatic
    primitive) and Branch~III (physical observables).
    It takes from Branch~I the Born--Infeld admissibility constraint and, as a supplied
    input, a Heisenberg carrier --- the Heisenberg--Schr\"{o}dinger representation $\pi_c$ of
    $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ on $\mathcal{H}_c$, whose associated Weil action is a further
    representation of the symplectic automorphism group (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}) --- and
    aims to produce the effective Lorentzian metric used throughout Branch~III; it currently
    yields, conditionally, a rank-three co-metric (Section~\ref{sec:status}).

    \medskip
    Its relation to the spectral admissibility sub-programme (Presentation Note~1) is the
    following.
    Note~1 records, as supplied inputs, that the admissible sector is taken to be carried
    by the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ carrier $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ and that
    $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ is a supplied selection rule, matching the adjoint dimension
    $\Im\mathbb{H} \cong \su(2)$ of that carrier.
    The emergent geometry sub-programme takes these supplied inputs and asks: what metric
    structure do they support?

    \medskip
    \noindent\textit{Notation.}
    In this note $\mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ denotes the $q$-dimensional supplied
    Heisenberg carrier, and $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ denotes the supplied spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
    carrier of Note~1, so that $\Sym^2(V_\rho) \cong \mathbb{C}^3$.
    This convention is local to the note and establishes no derived relation between the two
    carriers; companion papers use the symbol $V_\rho$ differently (Foundation~M writes it for the
    Weil module built on $\mathcal{H}_c$).

    \begin{remark}[Vocabulary note]
Throughout this note, the term \emph{admissibility constraint} replaces the earlier
``relaxation'' vocabulary of the preliminary Cosmochrony formulation.
The substrate $\chi$ is static: no substrate dynamics or relaxation mechanism is
postulated at the foundational level.
Observable evolution is an induced ordering of admissible projections, not a dynamics of
$\chi$ itself.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Scope: metric reconstruction only]
This sub-programme organises the chain $\Pi_q \to \geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}$, now
conditional on [H-L] at its first link and incomplete at its last: the rank-four extension is
open (Q5b-O2) and no coefficient value is derived.
It does not address the dynamics of $\geff$: the question of whether, and under which inputs, the effective
metric satisfies the Einstein equations is handled by the spectral gravity sub-programme, which
takes the metric targeted here as its starting point.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Logical Chain of the Sub-Programme}
    \label{sec:chain}

    The sub-programme is organised around the following derivation chain.

    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \underbrace{\Pi_q}_{\text{admissibility filter}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\Lpi = -A\partial_x^2}_{\substack{\text{1D shadow} \\ \text{(now [H-L])}}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\Leff \text{ on } \mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})}_{\substack{\text{4D effective operator} \\ \text{(Q5b; [H-L], [H-lift],} \\ \text{supplied $\tau$ term)}}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\geff \propto A_{\mu\nu}}_{\substack{\text{metric extraction} \\ \text{(Q5b;} \\ \text{[H-L], [H-lift])}}}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}}_{\substack{\text{metric closure (target)} \\ \text{(Q8, Q11)}}}.
    \end{equation}

    The last link presupposes a rank-four extension of the rank-three co-metric of Q5b, which is
    not constructed (open problem Q5b-O2); it holds only for an extension satisfying the
    hypotheses of Q11 Corollary~6.1 ([T] and [INV$^+$] of Q8, [ACT$_\tau$] invariance and a negative ordering term), after rescaling
    $\tau$, with an overall scale that no source fixes (Section~\ref{sec:q7q11}).
    A rank-four Lorentzian extension alone does not suffice: the extension
    $\mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 3)$ of the Q5b block has central entry different from the horizontal
    one, and no rescaling of $\tau$ makes it proportional to $\eta^{\mu\nu}$.
    The chain has five conceptually distinct stages, each resolved by a distinct group of papers.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textit{Discrete-to-continuum.}
      Earlier versions of Q5a (up to 2.1) claimed the Mosco convergence~\cite{Mosco1994} of the admissibility
      Dirichlet forms $\mathcal{E}_q$ on $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ to a one-dimensional shadow
      operator $\Lpi = -A\partial_x^2$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
      Q5a version 3.0 withdraws this derivation and leaves Q5 open: the canonical filtration
      is exactly a growing toric Fourier window, the published form converges to the zero
      form (assuming uniformly bounded weights), and, under the hypotheses [H-depth] and [H-$w'$] of Q5a Theorem~6.1, no common
      scalar normalisation yields a non-trivial toric differential operator.
      This link is now the explicit hypothesis [H-L] (Q5b version 2.0).
      Q5a-O2 establishes the pure-Fourier-mode structure of the pre-saturation pipeline and the
      truncation status of its finite-rank reduction, and rules out the $q^{-1}$ coercivity
      scale; hypotheses [H-E1] and Conjecture~[C] of the withdrawn framework are not closed by
      that route, their disposition being the zero-form Mosco limit of Q5a version 3.0.
      H2 closes hypothesis~[H2] of the same framework.

      \item \textit{Dimensional promotion.}
      The Carnot convergence of BFS shells and the Bass--Guivarc'h homogeneous
      dimension~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973}
      $\Dhom = 4$ give the spatial limit the homogeneous geometry of a four-dimensional space
      (Q5b Theorems~2.1 and~3.2; structural).
      The four-dimensional effective operator
      $\Leff = -\Atau\partial_\tau^2 + \AH\Delta_H$ on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$
      does not follow from these results: Q5b poses it under [H-L] and [H-lift], with a supplied
      temporal term and stipulated signs (Q5b Section~5.1 and Theorem~5.2).

      \item \textit{Metric extraction.}
      Q5b Theorem~5.2, conditional on [H-L] and on the open lifting hypothesis [H-lift],
      extracts the effective co-metric tensor $\geff \propto A_{\mu\nu}$ from the principal
      symbol of $\Leff$; it has rank three, with an empty central slot.
      Q5b Theorem~6.1, conditional in addition on the hyperbolicity hypothesis
      [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] applied to the reduction of $\Leff$ to functions independent of the
      central variable, gives signature $(-,+,+)$ on the non-degenerate block; a diagonal
      extension with positive central entry would have signature $(-,+,+,+)$, and constructing
      it is the open problem Q5b-O2.

      \item \textit{Coefficient determination.}
      Under the supplied identification [ID] of the effective projection space with the
      spin-one module $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ and a supplied spatial action [ACT], Q7 shows that an
      equivariant bridge $\Sym^2(V_\rho) \to W_{\mathrm{sp}} \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C}$ is zero
      or an isomorphism, unique up to one non-zero complex scalar; its geometric target remains
      to be constructed (Q7 Conjecture~4.7).
      For a supplied positive rank-three target with full $\su(2)$-invariance, Q8 gives
      $\AH = \AZ = 2\lambda$ with $\lambda > 0$ free; no value of $\AZ$ is derived.
      Q10 bounds the ratio $\AH/\AZ$ under spectral universality~[U] and three further inputs
      that are not supplied, and derives no value.
      [U] is open: U1 withdraws its former proof and proves an equidistance obstruction.

      \item \textit{Metric closure.}
      Q11 shows that invariance under a spatial $\SU(2)$ action leaves the temporal
      coefficient $\Atau$ independent of the spatial scale, so $\Atau$ is not derived.
      For a supplied rank-four extension satisfying [T] and [INV$^+$] of Q8, [ACT$_\tau$] invariance and a negative ordering term
      (Q11 Corollary~6.1; Q5b-O2 open), the co-metric is
      $\mathrm{diag}(-\Atau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda)$, and $\geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}$
      after rescaling $\tau$, with its overall scale free.
      Hypothesis [H-w] is open (W1 version 2.0.1).
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Constituent Papers}

    \subsection{Continuum limit and one-dimensional shadow: Q5a and Q5a-O2}
      \label{sec:q5a}

      \textbf{Q5a}~\cite{Beau2026q5a}, in its versions up to 2.1, claimed, conditionally on
      hypotheses [H1], [H-w], [H-E1], and Conjecture~[C], three convergences: a Hilbert
      inductive limit $\varinjlim_q \mathcal{C}_q \simeq L^2(\mathbb{R})$ (T1), convergence of
      rescaled Weil generators to the metaplectic generators of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ (T2), and
      Mosco convergence of the admissibility Dirichlet forms $\mathcal{E}_q$ to the
      second-order operator $\Lpi = -A\partial_x^2$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, $A > 0$ (T3).

      Q5a version 3.0 (\emph{Canonical Fourier Filtration and the Obstruction to a Spatial
      Continuum Limit}) withdraws this framework and replaces it with three established
      results: an exact identification (the canonical filtration stage is precisely the toric
      Fourier window $\mathrm{span}\{e^{2\pi i b x/q} : |b| \le n\}$), a zero-form theorem
      (assuming uniformly bounded weights, which is numerically supported but not proved, the
      published admissibility form converges to the zero form on this filtration, at rate
      $q^{-2}$), and a normalisation no-go (under a diverging saturation depth and the measured
      weight behaviour, that is, hypotheses [H-depth] and [H-$w'$] of Q5a Theorem~6.1, no common
      scalar normalisation of the form produces a non-trivial toric differential operator).
      The only non-trivial rescaled limit is a conditional Dirichlet operator in the rescaled
      \emph{frequency} variable, which does not provide a spatial continuum.
      Consequently, no operator $-A\partial_x^2$ on a spatial $L^2$ space is derived anywhere
      in the corpus; the existence of such an operator is the explicit hypothesis [H-L]
      (Q5b version 2.0), and Q5 is open.

      The O-series link is structural: Hypothesis~[H-E1] (uniform spectral gap) is equivalent to the
      stabilisation of $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ across primes, supported by the O12--O25 campaign.
      Hypothesis~[H-w] (admissibility weight convergence $a_q(s) \to A > 0$) is open:
      W1~\cite{Beau2026w1} version 2.0.1 withdraws its former proof.

      \textbf{Q5a-O2}~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2} gives the exact Fourier description of the
      pre-saturation pipeline and the epistemic status of its finite-rank reduction.
      BFS fingerprints and selected Gram--Schmidt vectors are pure Fourier modes, with $o(q)$
      displacements (frequencies $o(q)$ for fixed block parameters, macroscopic per-$q$ under
      the implemented sampling, with no $q$-independent limit); the three-coordinate reduction
      is a truncation fixed in advance ($\mathrm{HEFF\_DIM}=3$), from which no admissible
      rank can be measured; and the admissibility form is $O(q^{-2})$ on the whole unit sphere
      (a normalisation-prefactor fact), which rules out the $q^{-1}$ coercivity scale.
      Hypotheses [H-E1] and [C] of the former framework are therefore not closed by a
      finite-rank route; their disposition is the zero-form Mosco limit on the canonical
      Fourier-window filtration established by Q5a version 3.0.

    \subsection{Carnot geometry and Lorentzian signature: Q5b}
      \label{sec:q5b}

      \textbf{Q5b}~\cite{Beau2026q5b} delivers three results: a structural one, the Carnot
      convergence, and two conditional ones, the metric extraction and the signature.

      \textit{Carnot convergence} (Theorem~3.2; structural).
      The BFS shell stratification $\{S_n\}$ of $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
      converges, in the pre-saturation regime $n \ll q$, to the Carnot--Carath\'{e}odory
      sphere foliation of $\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$.
      The homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ (Bass--Guivarc'h) gives the limiting geometry
      the spectral and volume-growth properties of a four-dimensional space.
      This result is structural: it follows from Pansu's asymptotic cone theorem~\cite{Pansu1983} and requires
      no new hypothesis.

      \textit{Metric extraction} (Theorem~5.2; conditional on [H-L] and [H-lift]).
      Under [H-L] and the open lifting hypothesis [H-lift] (Q5b Hypothesis~4.1), $\Lpi$ is the image,
      under the Schr\"{o}dinger representation $\pi_1$, of the kinetic sector of the
      sub-Riemannian Laplacian $\Delta_H = \tilde{X}^2 + \tilde{Y}^2$ on $\Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$,
      restricted to the admissible sector.
      The effective co-metric $\geff \propto A_{\mu\nu}$ is extracted from the principal symbol
      of the resulting effective operator $\Leff$ on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \Heis_3(\mathbb{R})$.
      It is $\mathrm{diag}(-\Atau, \AH, \AH, 0)$ in the left-invariant frame: it has rank three,
      the central slot is empty and no term of lower differential order fills it, so a
      non-degenerate co-metric requires a new operator (open problem Q5b-O2).

      \textit{Lorentzian signature} (Theorem~6.1; conditional on Theorem~5.2 and on [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$]).
      Since the co-metric has rank three, the hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp] of the conditional
      signature analysis of~\cite{Beau2026c} (Section~7) cannot be applied to $\Leff$ itself; it is applied to the
      reduction $L_{\mathrm{red}} = -\Atau\partial_\tau^2 + \AH(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2)$ of
      $\Leff$ to functions independent of the central variable.
      This is [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$]: $L_{\mathrm{red}}$ is second order in $\tau$, its principal symbol
      is non-degenerate, and its propagation has a finite characteristic cone with $\tau$ as the
      single ordering direction.
      The hypothesis is explicit; it is not derived from the Born--Infeld capacity bound, which
      alone does not force hyperbolicity.
      In homogeneous and quasi-isotropic admissible regimes and under [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$], the
      non-degenerate block $(-\Atau, \AH, \AH)$ has signature $(-,+,+)$ with $\tau$ time-like:
      definite forms are excluded by the finite-cone clause, a spatial time-like direction by the
      ordering clause, and degenerate or parabolic symbols by the non-degeneracy clause.
      The signs of $\Leff$ are stipulated, not derived; the theorem records that they are the
      only ones compatible with a finite characteristic cone ordered by $\tau$ (Q5b Remark~6.2).
      A diagonal extension $\mathrm{diag}(-\Atau, \AH, \AH, A_z)$ with $A_z > 0$ would have
      signature $(-,+,+,+)$; constructing such an extension is the open problem Q5b-O2.

      After normalising the maximal admissible propagation speed to $c = 1$, the non-degenerate
      block is proportional to $\mathrm{diag}(-1, +1, +1)$ in the homogeneous isotropic
      regime.

    \subsection{Effective spacetime output: Q6b}
      \label{sec:q6b}

      \textbf{Q6b}~\cite{Beau2026q6b} is the integrative output of the sub-programme.
      Its role is not to re-derive the geometric convergence theorems of Q5a--Q5b, but to assemble
      the chain into the macroscopic effective-spacetime statement:
      \[
        \Pi_q \;\longrightarrow\; \Leff \;\longrightarrow\; \geff \;\longrightarrow\; G_{\mu\nu}.
      \]

      Its version~2.0 states three conditional results.
      (i)~For a supplied rank-four extension that is spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat in
      the exterior of a localised obstruction, flux conservation gives, when the exterior is asymptotically
      homogeneous ($A_r \to 1$), the leading Newtonian profile $\Phi_0 - C/r$; the Schwarzschild form follows
      only with exterior homogeneity $A_r \equiv 1$ and, as external dynamical input, the closure $f_t f_r = 1$
      and the exact temporal identification, and no uniqueness is claimed (Q6b Proposition~3.2).
      No extension diagonal in the left-invariant frame with $A_z > 0$ qualifies, and a qualifying extension
      would not preserve the large-scale Carnot structure; whether the admissibility structure supplies one is
      open.
      (ii)~The horizon degeneracy of that conditional exterior is chart-relative, and its structural
      reading is an interpretation.
      (iii)~The relation to the Einstein equations is conditional: the Einstein coefficient of the
      Gravity paper is a matching datum on Riemannian backgrounds, and the field equations of the
      spectral-stationarity paper~\cite{Beau2026Thermodynamics} assume an infrared response and a matter
      functional; reading them as consistency conditions of the emergent geometry is an interpretation.

      In Q6b the chain $\Pi_q \to \Leff \to \geff \to G_{\mu\nu}$ is conditional at each arrow: on [H-L],
      on [H-lift], on a supplied rank-four extension, and on the matching data of the Gravity paper together with
      a Lorentzian formulation that is not available.
      Since Q6b inherits the metric of Q5b Theorem~5.2, its results are conditional on the open
      lifting hypothesis [H-lift]; Q9 does not discharge it (Section~\ref{sec:q7q11}).
      Its Schwarzschild and Einstein statements also require a non-degenerate four-dimensional
      Lorentzian metric, that is, a supplied rank-four extension of the rank-three co-metric,
      whose construction Q6b itself assigns to the open problem Q5b-O2; they inherit the
      signature hypothesis [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] of Q5b Theorem~6.1.
      The further conditionality is carried by the spatial limit hypothesis [H-L]
      (Q5b version 2.0), which Q5a version 3.0 shows to fail for the published normalisation
      and its common scalar rescalings; [H-w], a component of the withdrawn framework, is open
      (W1 version 2.0.1), while [H-E1] and [C] are not closed by the finite-rank route of
      Q5a-O2.

      \begin{remark}[Status of Q6b]
Q6b is listed as published in the programme.
Its status as \emph{integrative output} means that it assembles rather than proves: it
inherits the metric from Q5b and relates it, conditionally, to the Einstein equations of the Gravity and
spectral-stationarity papers.
It should not be cited as the primary source for either the metric or the Einstein
equations; those primary sources are Q5b, and the Gravity and spectral-stationarity papers, respectively.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{Spatial, vertical, horizontal, and temporal coefficients: Q7--Q11}
      \label{sec:q7q11}

      Papers Q7--Q11 examine what representation theory and fibre uniformity can contribute to
      the coefficients of the effective co-metric.
      None of them derives a coefficient value.
      The $\su(2)$ Casimir acts as $2 \cdot \mathrm{Id}$ on the spin-one module for generators
      normalised by $[J_1, J_2] = iJ_3$, but the invariant forms on that module form one ray,
      the Casimir form is the point of that ray fixed by this normalisation, and rescaling the
      generators by $\kappa$ multiplies it by $\kappa^2$ (Q8 Lemma~4.1).

      \textbf{Q7}~\cite{Beau2026q7} (version 2.0) states what an equivariant bridge would
      require and proves three requirements.
      First, $\su(2) \not\cong \heis_3(\mathbb{R})$ as Lie algebras (Q7 Proposition~3.1), so a
      bridge can operate only at the representation level.
      Second, under the identification hypothesis [ID], which is supplied and not derived (O29
      identifies no such structure), the effective projection space is the spin-one module
      $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$, the unique irreducible three-dimensional $\su(2)$-module (Q7
      Proposition~4.1).
      Third, under an adjoint action [ACT] on the spatial sector, which no source supplies, an
      equivariant map $\Sym^2(V_\rho) \to W_{\mathrm{sp}} \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C}$ is zero or
      an isomorphism, unique up to one non-zero complex scalar (Q7 Lemma~4.3); nothing excludes
      the zero map or makes the scalar real and positive, so no metric coefficient is determined.
      The reading of the three directions of $\Im\mathbb{H} \cong \su(2)$ as spatial is part of
      that missing bridge (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}).
      The commutation $[F_c, L_{\mathrm{Weil}}] = 0$ holds (Q7 Proposition~6.1), but its
      application to the O25 data is withdrawn, and no statement about cross terms of the
      principal symbol of $\Leff$ is established.
      The former numerical coefficients are compressions of the discrete Weil Laplacian onto pure
      Fourier modes: the central value $2$ is the flat-mode Rayleigh quotient $4 - 2 - 0$, which
      is not normalisation-invariant and carries no evidential weight, and the horizontal value
      is $4 - 2\cos(2\pi k/q)$ for the mode index selected by the pipeline (Q7 Remark~6.3).
      The geometric target is a conjecture: a spatial construction beyond the rank-two symbol of
      Q5b that supplies a positive rank-three form with [ACT], [INV] and [ADAPT] (Q7
      Conjecture~4.7).

      It gives a sufficient condition for generator suppression: if the kinetic forms
      Mosco-converge in a specified Hilbert-space comparison~\cite{KuwaeShioya2003}~[K] and each finite-energy limit
      vector admits a kinetic recovery sequence on which the modulation energy vanishes~[R], the
      modulation energy leaves the kinetic Mosco limit unchanged, and on any supplied
      Schr\"{o}dinger fibre the limit is the free kinetic form, not the full oscillator form
      (Q9 Theorem~1.2 and Corollary~6.1).
      Neither [K] nor [R] is derived from the admissibility data, and the representation, the
      admissible domain and the geometric lift remain supplied.
      A supplied relative position-moment bound gives only an elementwise kinetic lower bound on
      a cone (Q9 Corollary~5.2); it establishes no bridge non-obstruction.
      Q5b Theorems~5.2 and~6.1 therefore remain conditional on [H-lift].

      \textbf{Q8}~\cite{BeauQ8} (version 2.0) proves that the Heisenberg commutator
      $[\tilde{X}, \tilde{Y}] = \tilde{Z}$ does not supply a central coefficient: the principal
      symbol of $-\Delta_H$ has rank two at every point, no term of lower differential order
      changes it, and a constant central coefficient requires a term $\tilde{Z}^2$ of homogeneous
      degree four, so that equality of the central and horizontal coefficients selects a length
      scale (Q8 Theorem~1.1).
      For a supplied positive rank-three target [T] invariant under the full $\su(2)$ action
      [INV$^+$], it gives $\AH = \AZ = 2\lambda$ with $\lambda > 0$ not determined (Q8
      Proposition~4.2); the Casimir fixes the normalisation of one invariant form, not a
      coefficient.
      No value of $\AZ$ is derived: a value would require a dimensionful datum of the
      construction, obtained independently of $\AH$ (Q8, Section~6.3).
      This does not close open problem Q5b-O2: the argument concerns a supplied positive
      rank-three form invariant under the full $\su(2)$ action of the spin-one module, and it
      does not construct the rank-four extension, whose central entry requires a new operator
      (Q5b Remark~5.4; Q5b-O2 open).

      spectral universality hypothesis~[U] with rate $\varepsilon(q) < 1$, a sector assignment
      [W], a response law [R] and a supplied target [T],
      $|\AH/\AZ - 1| \leq 2\varepsilon(q)/(1 - \varepsilon(q))$, and an absolute value follows
      only from a separately supplied scale [S] (Q10 Theorem~1.1).
      These inputs are named and not supplied, [U] is open, and no value of $\AH$ or $\AZ$ is
      derived; invariance fixes an invariant form only up to a positive factor, so the Casimir
      value 2 determines no coefficient.
      The former numerical fit $|A_H(q) - 2| \approx 0.264\,q^{-0.44}$ is withdrawn: for the

      obstruction: for an odd prime $q$, the multiplication generators at any two distinct
      central characters are at operator distance exactly $2\cos(\pi/2q)$, so no modulus of
      continuity in the character follows from individual generators, and the summed operator
      has no uniform modulus either.
      Hypothesis~[U], its rate and any identification of $\AH$ are open.

      \textbf{Q11}~\cite{Beau2026q11} (version 2.0) determines what invariance under a spatial
      $\SU(2)$ action can contribute to the temporal coefficient.
      Every invariant symmetric bilinear form on the cotangent fibre is
      $\alpha\,k_\tau^2 + \beta\,Q_{\mathrm{sp}}$ with $\alpha$ and $\beta$ independent: invariance
      excludes mixed temporal--spatial terms but fixes no ratio, and a spatial normalisation
      yields no value of $\Atau$ (Q11 Theorem~1.1).
      For a supplied full-rank extension satisfying [T] and [INV$^+$] of Q8, invariant under
      [ACT$_\tau$] and with a negative ordering term, the co-metric in an adapted frame is

        \label{eq:metric}
        \geff = \mathrm{diag}(-\Atau, 2\lambda, 2\lambda, 2\lambda),
        \qquad \Atau > 0,\ \lambda > 0 \text{ independent},
      \end{equation}
      non-degenerate of rank four and of signature $(-,+,+,+)$; rescaling $\tau$, a choice of
      temporal unit, turns it into $2\lambda\,\eta^{\mu\nu}$ with
      $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1, +1, +1, +1)$ (Q11 Corollary~6.1).
      The construction of such an extension is the open problem Q5b-O2, and Q5b Theorem~5.2
      gives rank three.
      The overall scale is free: setting $\AH = 2$ or $\Atau = 2$ is a choice of units, not a
      consequence of invariance (Q11).
      The missing element is one datum coupling the temporal and spatial normalisations, such as
      the step $\Delta\tau$ expressed in spatial units.

  \section{Inputs from Upstream Results}
    \label{sec:inputs}

    The emergent geometry sub-programme takes the following inputs from Branch~I and from
    Presentation Note~1 (spectral admissibility).

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Supplied Heisenberg carrier on $L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.}
      The carrier is supplied, not selected: the admissibility axioms together with
      Born--Infeld parity do not select a finite Heisenberg group or its carrier, as an explicit
      $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel shows (HeisenbergStructure Theorem~4.1).
      Once a finite Heisenberg group and a non-trivial central character are supplied,
      the finite Stone--von Neumann theorem~\cite{GurevichHadani2009} fixes the
      Heisenberg--Schr\"{o}dinger representation $\pi_c$ on
      $\mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ uniquely up to unitary equivalence.
      Identifying the admissible fibre with $\mathcal{H}_c$ is a modelling dictionary, not a
      consequence of the axioms~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
      The associated Weil action is a further representation of the symplectic automorphism
      group, not the Heisenberg representation itself.

      \item \textbf{Born--Infeld admissibility constraint.}
      The bounded-flux condition $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ is the Q5a starting point.
      Its origin in the BI action is established in Branch~I.

      \item \textbf{Spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector and $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$.}
      From Presentation Note~1 (O23, O27, O29), as supplied inputs: the admissible sector is
      taken to be carried by $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ (a supplied carrier, not derived);
      the threshold $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ is a supplied selection rule, matching the adjoint
      dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \Im\mathbb{H} = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \su(2) = 3$ of that
      carrier (O23, Theorem~3.1); the reading of these three directions as spatial is itself
      a bridge under construction in this branch, not an algebraic fact.
      Q7 takes the identification of the effective projection space with $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ as the
      supplied hypothesis [ID]; these inputs do not derive it (O29 identifies no such
      structure).

      \item \textbf{O-series universality data.}
      The stabilisation of $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}(q)$ across the O12--O25 campaign provides
      numerical support for the spectral universality [U] required by Q10; [H-E1] itself is open
      (its $q^{-1}$ scale is ruled out on the canonical filtration).
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Open Deliverables}
    \label{sec:open}

    Three open items define the boundary of the sub-programme as of this note.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 0: the spatial limit input [H-L].}
    Establishing --- or replacing --- the spatial limit operator $\Lpi = -A\partial_x^2$
    consumed by the metric chain.
    Q5a version 3.0 shows that, under the unproved hypotheses [H-depth] and [H-$w'$] (Q5a
    Theorem~6.1), the published admissibility form does not produce it under any common scalar
    normalisation; the conditional dual-window Dirichlet limit is a dual-space
    statement and does not provide a spatial continuum.
    This is the open content of Q5 and the principal obstruction of the sub-programme.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 1: an extended spatial target and the coefficient data.}

    scale (Q8-O3) and the temporal-to-spatial ratio (Q11).
    No coefficient value is established in the meantime; an equivariant bridge at finite $q$ is
    part of this construction.

    \textbf{Open deliverable 2: [H1] on the full $L^2$ space.}
    Q5a-O2 closes [H1] on the admissible sector.
    The full-space version is not needed by any result in the programme and is listed here for

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The emergent geometry sub-programme organises the conditional reconstruction of an
    effective Lorentzian metric from the admissibility filter $\Pi_q$ acting on a supplied
    Heisenberg carrier, the Heisenberg--Schr\"{o}dinger representation of
    $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ on $\mathcal{H}_c$.
    Its target, a co-metric $\geff \propto \eta^{\mu\nu}$, presupposes a rank-four extension that
    is not constructed (Q5b-O2), and its overall scale is not fixed.

    The chain has five stages, each addressed by distinct papers: the discrete-to-continuum
    stage, now the open hypothesis [H-L] after the Q5a version 3.0 withdrawal (Q5a, Q5a-O2,
    W1, H2); the dimensional promotion via Carnot geometry (Q5b);
    the metric extraction and signature selection (Q5b, conditional on [H-lift]); the analysis of
    the metric coefficients $\AZ$, $\AH$, $\Atau$ (Q7, Q8, Q10, Q11, U1), which derives no
    coefficient value; and the assembly into the effective spacetime statement (Q6b).
    On a supplied rank-four extension with the hypotheses of Q8 and of Q11 Corollary~6.1, the spatial
    coefficients are equal and the co-metric is proportional to $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ after a choice
    of temporal unit; the common spatial scale and the ratio of the temporal and spatial
    normalisations are free.
    The value 2 is the eigenvalue of the $\su(2)$ Casimir on the spin-one module
    $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ in one normalisation of the generators, not a coefficient.

    Under [H-L] and [H-lift], Q5b extracts a rank-three co-metric; under [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] in
    addition, its non-degenerate block has signature $(-,+,+)$, the stipulated signs being the
    only ones compatible with a $\tau$-ordered finite characteristic cone (Q5b Remark~6.2).
    The reconstruction problem is not closed: the rank-four extension is open (Q5b-O2), and no
    metric coefficient value is derived.
    Without [H-L], the geometric convergence results and the algebraic statements of Q7 and Q8
    stand, and the discrete-to-continuum stage is open: this is the current state of Q5.
    The overall scale of the co-metric and the temporal-to-spatial ratio require data that no
    source supplies (Q8, Q11).

    This note does not address the dynamics of $\geff$.
    The question of whether, and under which inputs, the effective metric satisfies the Einstein equations is
    handled by the
    spectral gravity sub-programme (Presentation Note~4), which works on a supplied Riemannian metric; its
    reading as the effective metric of this note is conditional on [H-L], [H-lift] and a supplied rank-four
    extension, and relating it to a Lorentzian metric needs a continuation that is not performed.

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
    \emph{This paragraph is a reading, not a result.}
    Read conditionally, the sub-programme locates where an emergent spacetime would have to come
    from.
    The Carnot convergence and $\Dhom = 4$ are structural; every metric statement waits on
    [H-L] and [H-lift]; the Lorentzian reading of the non-degenerate block needs
    [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$]; a four-dimensional co-metric needs an extension that no source constructs
    (Q5b-O2), and proportionality to $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ needs in addition the hypotheses of Q11
    Corollary~6.1 and a choice of temporal unit; even then, the overall scale and the temporal-to-spatial
    ratio remain independent data (Q8, Q11).
    On this reading, the representation theory of the supplied carriers fixes shapes, not
    sizes: the numbers that would make the effective geometry physical would have to be supplied
    by the admissible structure itself, not by symmetry.
